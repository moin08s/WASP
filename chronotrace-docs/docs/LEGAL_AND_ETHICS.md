# Legal and Ethics

ChronoTrace automates digital forensic analysis — file metadata extraction, system
artefact extraction, timestamp extraction, chronological timeline reconstruction, SHA-256
evidence integrity, and structured investigation reports — and is intended solely for
lawful digital forensics, incident response, authorized investigations, and research on
systems and data the examiner is permitted to examine.

## Authorization

Before acquisition or analysis, establish the applicable authority, scope, retention requirements, and handling restrictions. Record the authorization reference in the case metadata where appropriate.

Do not use ChronoTrace to access, acquire, monitor, or disclose data without appropriate authorization.

## Privacy and minimization

Forensic evidence may contain credentials, communications, personal information, and unrelated sensitive data. Limit collection and disclosure to the authorized scope. Use redaction profiles for reports when full values are not required.

## Evidence integrity

Never modify source evidence for convenience. Preserve acquisition hashes, manifests, custody records, parser versions, warnings, and methodology notes so another examiner can understand and reproduce the examination.

## Reporting ethics

Distinguish observed facts from inference. Preserve raw timestamps and source provenance, disclose parser limitations and confidence, and avoid presenting a single artefact as conclusive when it only corroborates a hypothesis.

## Responsible disclosure

Do not publish real case data, personal information, credentials, or sensitive evidence in issues, examples, fixtures, or documentation. Use synthetic or properly licensed public forensic corpora.

## Jurisdiction

Legal requirements vary by jurisdiction and matter. This document is project guidance, not legal advice. Examiners remain responsible for following the laws, warrants, policies, professional standards, and disclosure obligations applicable to their work.
